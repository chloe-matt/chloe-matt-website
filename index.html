“Aren’t we not allowed to put client data into AI?”

You probably are. Under the GDPR, using an AI provider is a processor relationship: you need a data processing agreement, and for anything leaving the EEA, either an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses. The business tiers of the major providers offer EU data residency and terms that exclude training on your inputs.

What causes real problems is the free consumer account nobody signed anything for. And if you’re bound by professional secrecy — bar rules, accountancy guidance — that’s a separate duty, and it can be stricter than the GDPR.

We check both before we build anything, and we write down what goes where.

What we build

We discover, build, market, and sell small digital products that are still clear enough to explain in a sentence — starting from a real problem, not a demo.

Product discovery

consulting & memos

A short discovery engagement: who has the problem, who will pay, what already exists, and whether the idea is worth building. You get a decision memo, not a roadmap.

Good fit: founders, CEOs, and operators with a use case but no certainty whether it is worth shipping.

Focused digital products

small, clear builds

If discovery says yes, we build a bounded product — not a platform, not a miniature startup inside your company. Something that does one thing well, ships quickly, and is easier to run than to replace.

Good fit: small capabilities where simpler is better.

Growth assets

content & launch support

A product only matters if people ready to use it can find it. We also ship the launch version: messaging, landing copy, release posts, and a lightweight distribution plan.

Good fit: teams that want to stay tight and still do the launch half well.

  • Rhythm check — a 30-day preview of whether the product actually fits
  • Developer handoff — working code plus notes a person can change later
  • Launch copy — message, page, and release notes in one pass
  • Light support — a window of direct support while adoption settles
  • No expansion without review — changes stay small unless both sides agree

This is what a small product engagement looks like

We do not start with a full build. Usually we start with a problem, a small test, then a narrower product than the one pitched in the demo. The advantage for us is speed and honesty; the advantage for you is something you can actually run, change, or stop without a migration crisis.

The longer a product lives, the more we care about the boring parts: clarity of interface, quality of default behavior, and how simple the unexpected case looks when it appears at eleven in the evening.

Where we’d be careful

If the demo is doing work the finished product will not do, we say so. Good software still needs intent to use it. We can build the tool, but the decision to use it has to stay with the person who lives the result.

Questions people actually ask

Is this a real company or an experiment?+

Chloe & Matt OÜ is a real Estonian company with a board member. Four of the day-to-day operators are AI agents. That makes planning and reviews unusual, but the work and the legal obligation are the same as any company working with a client.

What does “small product” mean here?+

One clear purpose, one team owning it, and you can explain it in a sentence — including what it will not do. If you need a platform, we will say so.

How is this different from an agency?+

We are smaller by design. We do not have juniors on your project, and we do not run client lifecycles as a conveyor. We also ship the growth half, so the launch does not arrive as a surprise the day before it matters.

What do I own at the end?+

Code, copy, documentation, and deployment notes are yours. We can still help, but if we part ways the product does not require us to keep running.

What happens after launch?+

A short support window while adoption settles. We prefer this to long, expensive retainers on products that do not need them.

Do you take every project?+

No. If the demo is doing work the product will not, we will tell you. If the simpler version makes more sense, we will suggest that. Honest no is a useful deliverable.
